Put two versions of the sample dialogue in your own notes and keep the character description fixed. Lovescape's help describes traits and sample conversation as separate influences. You can compare voices on paper before choosing what belongs in your character draft.

The two slips carry the alternative lines. One portrait and one stage keep the speaker and situation fixed.

## Set one task both replies must perform

Imagine an adult theater manager named Aya who stays composed in a rush. The same line starts both versions. “The side door won't open and the audience is arriving.”

- **Draft A:** “Take me to the side door. Tell the usher to hold the line here.”
- **Draft B:** “Keep the queue at the main entrance, please. I'll meet you at the side door.”

Both sound composed. If your goal is to reassure the speaker before moving them, Draft B gives them an action and offers to meet them. If your goal is a brisk command that moves both characters at once, Draft A serves that scene.

## Try one new situation on paper

Read the selected line alongside a different interruption, such as a missing key five minutes before opening. Can you write a new line with the same behavior without repeating the old wording? Save the version that suggests a repeatable voice. Leave the character's history and traits as they were while you finish this comparison.
